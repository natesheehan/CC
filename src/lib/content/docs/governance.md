# Governance

Concept Cartography is a shared space for building and discussing knowledge. Good governance keeps that space useful, welcoming, and legible as more people contribute.

## Shared principles

- **Clarity over volume.** Prefer a well-defined concept and a meaningful relation over many loosely connected entries.
- **Attribution matters.** Edits are associated with the person who made them, and the activity feed records how maps evolve.
- **Discussion belongs with the idea.** Use relation comments to explain, question, or challenge a connection without losing the context of the map.
- **Stewardship is collaborative.** Map creators own the maps they start, while contributors help improve the shared knowledge inside them.

## Caring for a map

Map creators are responsible for the shape and continued care of their maps. Contributors should preserve useful context when editing concepts or relations, and use descriptions and sources when they make an idea easier to understand.

If a relation is disputed, describe the disagreement in its comment thread. A visible, specific discussion is more useful than silently removing context.

## Moderation and trust

Concept Cartography uses lightweight named sign-in and an activity trail rather than pretending that knowledge work is anonymous. Treat other contributors as collaborators, give credit, and raise concerns with enough detail that someone else can understand the reasoning.
