# Get started

## How it works
Concept Cartography helps you draw a map of ideas. Each idea is a circle on the map, and the lines between them show how the ideas are connected. You can build a map on your own, or with a class, a study group, or a research team. You don't need any technical skills, and there is nothing to install. This guide walks you through your first map, one step at a time.

> **The two words to know**
>
> A **concept** is one idea, such as *Photosynthesis* or *Sunlight*. It appears as a circle on the map.
>
> A **link** connects two concepts and says how they are related, for example *Photosynthesis depends on Sunlight*. It appears as a line between the circles.

## 1. Sign in with your name

Click **Sign in** in the top-right corner and type the name you'd like other people to see. You don't need a password or an email address.

![The sign-in page, with the name Ada Lovelace typed into the Display name box](/images/docs/get-started/01-sign-in.png)
*Type a display name and press Continue.*

Your name appears next to everything you add or change, so everyone can see who contributed what. To sign in again later, type the same name.

## 2. Start a new map

Go to **Maps** and click **New map**. Give your map a name, and if you like, a short description of what it's about. Then click **Create map**.

![The New concept map window, with the name "The history of jazz" and a short description filled in](/images/docs/get-started/02-maps.png)
*A map needs a name. The description is optional, but it helps other people understand what the map is for.*

A map can be about anything you want to understand better: a topic from a course, a research question, a book you're reading, or a hobby.

## 3. Add your first concepts

On your new map, click **Add concept** in the Tools panel on the right, or press the **N** key. Only the name is required. You can also add:

- a **definition** that explains the idea in a sentence or two;
- **sources**, which are links to where the idea comes from, such as an article, a book, or a web page;
- an **example** that makes the idea concrete;
- a **quiz question** that someone could use to test their understanding.

![The Add a concept window, with the name "Stomata" and a definition filled in](/images/docs/get-started/03-add-concept.png)
*Adding a concept called "Stomata". You can fill in the other fields now or come back to them later.*

**Tip:** right-click an empty part of the map to add a concept at that exact spot.


## 4. Link concepts together

Links are what turn a list of ideas into a map. Click **Add link** in the Tools panel, or press **L**. Then:

1. choose the concept the link starts **from**;
2. choose the kind of **relation**;
3. search for the concept it goes **to**;
4. optionally, add a sentence explaining the link.

![The Link two concepts window, linking Respiration to Glucose with the relation "Depends on"](/images/docs/get-started/05-add-link.png)
*Before you save, the window shows how the link reads as a sentence: "Respiration depends on Glucose".*

Concept Cartography has eight built-in kinds of relation, each with its own colour:

- **Type of**: one idea is a kind of another. *A robin is a type of bird.*
- **Part of**: one idea is a piece of a larger whole. *Chlorophyll is part of a leaf.*
- **Produces**: one idea causes or creates another. *Photosynthesis produces oxygen.*
- **Counteracts**: one idea works against another.
- **Depends on**: one idea needs another. *Photosynthesis depends on sunlight.*
- **Similar to**, **Equivalent to**, and **Distinct from**: for comparing ideas.

Most links point one way and are drawn with an arrow. If a relationship works in both directions, choose **Multi-directional**. If none of the built-in relations fit, click **Manage relation types** to create your own for this map.

## 5. Arrange your map

As you add concepts, they appear on the map. Drag a circle to move it. The map saves your layout, so everyone who opens the map sees the same arrangement.

![The "How plants make food" map, with Photosynthesis in the middle, linked to Sunlight, Water, Carbon dioxide, Glucose, Oxygen, Chlorophyll and Respiration](/images/docs/get-started/04-map.png)
*A finished map. The Relation types list on the left explains what each line colour means.*

Some useful ways to find your way around:

- **Zoom** by scrolling, pinching with two fingers on a touch screen, or with the buttons in the bottom-right corner.
- **Move around** by dragging an empty part of the map.
- **Press 0** to fit the whole map on your screen.
- **Hover** over a concept to highlight the concepts it's linked to.
- **Search** for a concept by name with the box in the top-left, or press **/**.

If you ever forget a shortcut, click **How it works** at the top of any map.

## 6. Explore a concept

Double-click any concept to open its details. The panel shows the concept's definition, sources, examples, and quiz question, along with every other concept it's linked to.

![The details panel for Photosynthesis, showing its definition, a source link, an example, a quiz question, and its related concepts](/images/docs/get-started/06-concept.png)
*The details panel for Photosynthesis. Click any related concept to jump straight to it.*

From here you can **Edit** the concept, add a new link from it, or click the link icon next to its name to copy a web address that opens the map with this concept already selected. This is handy for sharing a specific idea in an email or a class chat.

## 7. Discuss a link

People don't always agree on how two ideas relate, and those disagreements are often where the real learning happens. Click any line on the map to open its discussion.

![A discussion on the link "Respiration counteracts Photosynthesis", where two people debate whether "counteracts" is the right word](/images/docs/get-started/07-comments.png)
*Two contributors discuss whether "counteracts" is the right way to describe a link.*

Comments stay attached to the link they're about, so anyone who looks at the link later can follow the reasoning. You can also copy a web address that opens this exact discussion.

## 8. See what has changed

Every map keeps a record of who did what, and when. Open **Activity feed** from the Tools panel to see it.

![The Activity panel, listing recent edits, comments and new links, each with the person's name and how long ago it happened](/images/docs/get-started/08-activity.png)
*The activity feed shows the latest changes first.*

This makes it easy to catch up on what others have added since your last visit, and it means every contribution is credited to the person who made it.

## 9. Search every concept

The **Concepts** page is a shared dictionary of every concept from every map. Search it before you add a new concept: someone may already have defined it, and you can build on their work instead of starting again.

![The Concept dictionary, showing the search "photo" and the matching concept Photosynthesis](/images/docs/get-started/09-directory.png)
*Searching the dictionary for "photo" finds Photosynthesis and shows which map it belongs to.*
