Concept Cartography is a free, open-source atlas for mapping how ideas connect. People build **concept maps** together: each concept carries a definition, a link to its source literature, an example and an optional quiz prompt, and concepts are linked by typed relations such as _type of_, _part of_, _depends on_ or _distinct from_.

The goal is to make the relationships between ideas visible, arguable and shareable, so that a field's vocabulary can be explored as a landscape rather than a list.

## What you can do here

- **Build maps.** Create a map, add concepts and draw typed relations between them. Every map is a live, force-directed network you can pan, zoom, rotate and rearrange.
- **Browse the dictionary.** The [concept dictionary](/concepts) gathers every entry across every map, and each entry has its own link you can share or cite.
- **Discuss connections.** Relations can carry comments, so disagreements about how two ideas relate have a place to live.
- **See who did what.** Every edit is attributed and appears in an activity log, and the [community page](/community) shows how the atlas is growing.

## An experimental project

Concept Cartography is in **active development**. Features, layouts and data structures may change as we learn from how people use it, and occasional rough edges are expected. We're committed to shipping a **stable build by March 2027**.

## Principles

- **Open by default.** The software is open source and the maps are made to be read, reused and built on.
- **Credit where it's due.** Edits are attributed so contributors are visible, and concepts point back to their original sources.
- **Collect as little as possible.** Signing in needs only a display name: no email address, no password, no tracking. See the [privacy policy](/privacy) for details.
